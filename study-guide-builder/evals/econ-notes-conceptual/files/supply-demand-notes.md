# Intro Microeconomics: Markets (Week 3 notes)

## 1. Demand
The **law of demand**: other things equal, when the price of a good rises, the quantity demanded falls. The **demand curve** slopes downward.

### 1.1 Movement vs shift
A change in the good's own price causes a **movement along** the curve. A change in a *determinant* causes the whole curve to **shift**. Determinants of demand: consumer income, prices of related goods (substitutes and complements), tastes, expectations, number of buyers. For a *normal good* demand rises with income; for an *inferior good* it falls.

## 2. Supply
The **law of supply**: other things equal, when price rises, quantity supplied rises. The supply curve slopes upward. Determinants of supply: input costs, technology, taxes and subsidies, expectations, number of sellers. Lower input costs shift supply right.

## 3. Market equilibrium
The **equilibrium** is where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. The price at that point is the **equilibrium (market-clearing) price**.

**Example 3.1.** Demand: Qd = 100 − 2P. Supply: Qs = 4P − 20. Setting Qd = Qs: 100 − 2P = 4P − 20, so 6P = 120, P* = 20 and Q* = 60.

If price is above equilibrium there is a **surplus** (Qs > Qd) and price falls; below equilibrium there is a **shortage** (Qd > Qs) and price rises.

### 3.2 Shifts
If demand rises to Qd = 130 − 2P: 130 − 2P = 4P − 20, so P* = 25 and Q* = 80. A rightward demand shift raises both price and quantity. A rightward supply shift lowers price and raises quantity.

## 4. Price controls
A **price ceiling** is a legal maximum price. If set below equilibrium it is binding and causes a shortage. In Example 3.1 a ceiling at P = 15 gives Qd = 70 and Qs = 40, a shortage of 30.
A **price floor** is a legal minimum. If set above equilibrium it is binding and causes a surplus. A floor at P = 25 gives Qs = 80 and Qd = 50, a surplus of 30.

## 5. Elasticity
**Price elasticity of demand** = (% change in quantity demanded) / (% change in price). Demand is *elastic* if |E| > 1, *inelastic* if |E| < 1, unit elastic if |E| = 1.

**Example 5.1.** Price rises from 10 to 12 (+20%) and quantity falls from 100 to 90 (−10%). |E| = 10/20 = 0.5, inelastic.

**Total revenue rule:** if demand is inelastic, a price increase raises total revenue (here 10 × 100 = 1000 rises to 12 × 90 = 1080); if elastic, a price increase lowers it. Factors: availability of substitutes, necessity vs luxury, time horizon, share of budget.

## 6. Surplus and welfare
**Consumer surplus** is the area between the demand curve and the price, up to the quantity traded: what buyers would have been willing to pay minus what they paid. **Producer surplus** is the area between the price and the supply curve.

**Example 6.1.** At P* = 20, Q* = 60: the demand curve hits the price axis at P = 50, so CS = ½ × 60 × (50 − 20) = 900. The supply curve hits the price axis at P = 5, so PS = ½ × 60 × (20 − 5) = 450. Total surplus = 1350. Price controls that reduce the quantity traded create a **deadweight loss**: surplus that no one gets.
